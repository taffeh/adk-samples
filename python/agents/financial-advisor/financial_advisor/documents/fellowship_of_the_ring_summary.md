# The Fellowship of the Ring — Plot Summary

Sixty years after the adventure in which he found a magic ring, Bilbo Baggins
celebrates his eleventy-first birthday in the Shire with a lavish party, then
slips on the ring and vanishes from his own celebration, leaving Bag End and
the ring to his young cousin and heir, Frodo. Gandalf the wizard, troubled by
how strongly Bilbo clung to the ring, spends years searching for its history.
When he returns, he confirms his fears: it is the One Ring, forged by the Dark
Lord Sauron to rule the other Rings of Power. Sauron has regained strength in
Mordor and is searching for it, and his servants have learned that it lies
with a hobbit named Baggins.

Frodo agrees to take the ring away from the Shire and sets out with his
gardener, Sam Gamgee, later joined by his friends Merry and Pippin. They are
hunted from the start by the Black Riders, the Nazgûl, Sauron's most feared
servants. The hobbits escape through the Old Forest, where they are rescued
from a malevolent willow tree and later from a barrow-wight by the mysterious
and cheerful Tom Bombadil, over whom the ring seems to have no power.

At the Prancing Pony inn in Bree, Gandalf fails to meet them as planned. They
instead meet Strider, a weathered Ranger who proves to be Aragorn, heir to the
ancient kings of Gondor and Arnor. He guides them through the wilderness
towards Rivendell. On the hill of Weathertop, the Nazgûl attack, and their
leader stabs Frodo with a cursed blade whose splinter works its way towards
his heart. In a desperate race, the elf-lord Glorfindel helps carry Frodo to
the Ford of Bruinen, where the river rises in a flood that sweeps the Riders
away just as Frodo collapses.

Frodo wakes in Rivendell, healed by Elrond, and is reunited with Bilbo and
Gandalf, who explains that he was held captive by the wizard Saruman, who has
turned traitor and seeks the ring for himself. At the Council of Elrond,
representatives of elves, dwarves and men debate what to do. They conclude the
ring cannot be hidden, used or safely kept: it must be destroyed in the fires
of Mount Doom, in Mordor, where it was made. Frodo volunteers to carry it.

Nine companions set out to oppose the nine Riders: Frodo, Sam, Merry and
Pippin; Gandalf; Aragorn; Boromir, son of the Steward of Gondor; Legolas, an
elf of Mirkwood; and Gimli, a dwarf. Blocked by snow on the mountain pass of
Caradhras, they take the dark road beneath the mountains through the ruined
dwarf kingdom of Moria. There they find the tomb of Gimli's kinsman, are
attacked by orcs and a cave troll, and finally face a Balrog, an ancient demon
of fire. Gandalf holds the bridge of Khazad-dûm against it so the others can
escape, and falls with it into the abyss.

Grief-stricken, the company reaches the elven forest of Lothlórien, ruled by
Galadriel and Celeborn. Galadriel lets Frodo see visions in her mirror and,
when he offers her the ring, resists its temptation and refuses it. She gives
each companion a gift, including a phial of starlight for Frodo, and they
continue south by boat along the great river Anduin.

At Parth Galen, the company must decide whether to go to Gondor or straight to
Mordor. Boromir, increasingly tempted by the ring, believes it could save his
people and tries to take it from Frodo by force. Frodo escapes by putting on
the ring and realises it will corrupt the fellowship if he stays. Boromir,
ashamed, returns to the others as orcs attack. Frodo decides to go on alone,
but Sam refuses to be left behind and the two set off by boat for Mordor
together, while the rest of the fellowship is scattered.
